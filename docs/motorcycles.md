# Motorcycles: VFR800FI 1999 and two-wheeled support

The [Honda VFR800FI 99 asset](../assets/library/motorcycles/vfr800fi-1999/README.md) is **drivable (phases 1 and 2)** as the `vfr800` preset: `presetVehicle('vfr800', id, position)`, like the car preset. Its asset manifest, rig file and technical data stay outside the preset loader (`asset.json`, `*.rig.json` and `*.specs.json` are skipped).

## Phase 1 (implemented)

- **Schema and validation** (`src/entity/vehicle/field.ts`, `vehicle.ts`): `vehicle.twoWheeled` declares a single-track vehicle with exactly two hubs (front, then rear), its own `rearWheelRadius`, the chassis-local `steeringAxis` (up the head stock) and `steerLimit`. Two-wheelers get a narrow minimum size (0.3 × 0.3 × 1 m instead of 1 × 0.3 × 2 m); two hubs without `twoWheeled` are rejected, and `twoWheeled` cannot be combined with trailers, aircraft, boats or carriers. `powertrain.maxRpm` and `shift.upshiftRpm` accept up to 20,000 rpm. Cars, trucks and trailers validate exactly as before.
- **Rig extraction** (`scripts/lib/vehicle-rig.mjs`): `wheel.front` / `wheel.rear` anchors become the two hubs.
- **Controller** (`src/simulation/vehicles/two-wheeled/`, package export `@nabla/engine/vehicles/two-wheeled`): one Rapier rigid body with two ray-cast wheels (front and rear radii). The handlebar turns about the steering axis (rate-limited, clamped to the lock); the front wheel's ground steer follows through the rake, tan(ground) = tan(bar)·cos(rake). A lean controller tracks the steady-turn lean tan(φ) = v²·tan(steer)/(g·L), capped at `maxLean`, with a roll-disturbance observer for tyre and gravity moments; at speed the reachable bar angle shrinks so full input asks for exactly `maxLean`. Below `balanceSpeed` a stiffer balance assist keeps a stopped, parked or crawling machine upright (`balanceAssist`, on by default; it applies a roll torque and never adds fake wheel contacts). Past `fallLean` the machine has fallen and the controller lets go until R (`recoverVehicle`) uprights it. The pure math lives in `balance.ts`.
- **Inputs:** W throttle; S is the **front brake** (there is no reverse gear); Space (handbrake) is the **rear brake**; A/D steer. The shared automatic gearbox and engine model (`drivetrain.ts`) drive the rear wheel; P/N/D, the start-up sequence and manual paddles work as on cars. (Phase 2 replaced the phase-1 wheelie guard, which cut drive while the front tyre was off the ground, with real pitch dynamics, and made S and Space combined brakes on the vfr800; see below.)
- **Presentation** (`src/render/vehicle-presentation/motorcycle-rig.ts`): `bindMotorcycleRig` turns the handlebar/fork about the head axis, slides the fork, swings the swingarm and shock, bends the chain and spins both wheels from `Simulation.twoWheeledPose(id)`. It replaces the asset-folder `vfr800fi-1999-controls.mjs` helper (same math).
- **Cameras:** `vehicleInfo(id)` reports `twoWheeled`, `lean` and `leanAllowance` (the fall threshold). The chase camera follows yaw and stays level; `GroundHeading` removes lean up to the allowance before its rollover tests and the flip cinematic ignores roll inside it, so cornering is never a rollover. The cockpit view still rolls with the chassis.
- **Sound** (`vehicle.audio`): no turbo; a short quiet click on every gear change (see [configuration](configuration.md)).

## Phase 2 (implemented)

- **Wheelies and stoppies from real pitch.** Drive and brake forces act at the tyre contacts of the Rapier body, so the load transfer m·g·b/h (wheelie) and g·a/h (stoppie) happens in the physics, not in a script. The rider is part of the vehicle mass (`twoWheeled.rider`), which puts the centre of mass where a ridden bike has it; the pure formulas live in `two-wheeled/pitch.ts`. The **pitch assist** (`twoWheeled.pitchAssist`, on by default) only acts while one wheel is off the ground: between the soft and the maximum angle it fades the drive (wheelie) or eases the front brake towards `stoppieMinBrake` (stoppie), looking `anticipation` seconds ahead; past the maximum a critically damped pitch torque pulls the wheel back down; a fast landing is cushioned (`landingRate`). With the rider centred the assist allows only `wheelieNeutralAngle` / `stoppieNeutralAngle` (a light front or rear), so a full-throttle start does not become a wheelie by itself; the full soft/maximum angles need the rider back (wheelie) or forward (stoppie). Turn either assist off (`wheelie: false`, `stoppie: false`) to ride without the net: the bike loops out or flips over the bar.
- **Rider counterweight** (`two-wheeled/rider.ts`). **U / O** hang off left / right, **I** moves the weight forward over the tank, **L** sits back. The rider moves at `rider.rate` and drags the body centre of mass with it (Rapier additional mass properties, inertia kept). Hanging off to the inside of a turn leans the bike less (the lean target adds atan(Δx/h)); a sideways shift also adds a little body steering (`rider.steer`); weight back makes wheelies easier and opens the wheelie assist, weight forward does the same for stoppies. The rider's head and the cockpit camera follow the shift (`riderHeadOffset` in the entity view).
- **Clutch kick.** Shift (the launch input) with the throttle open in first or second gear (`clutchKick.maxGear`) adds a short burst of drive (`gain`, `seconds`), so a wheelie can be popped in second gear too.
- **Dual CBS** (`two-wheeled/brakes.ts`, `twoWheeled.cbs`). With `cbs` declared, the lever (S) and the pedal (Space) each brake both wheels by configurable shares of `frontBrakeForce` / `rearBrakeForce`; the cross-linked circuits build up with a first-order lag (`linkLag`). Omit `cbs` for independent brakes (phase-1 behaviour). **The shares are TODO(unverified), not Honda data:** no Honda source with the hydraulic split was found. The default shares are piston counts only, from the [Wikipedia VFR800 article](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_VFR800#Dual_combined_braking_system_(CBS)>) (uncited there): the lever applies 4 of the 6 front pistons and, through the secondary master cylinder, 1 of the 3 rear pistons; the pedal the remaining pistons (2 rear and "one or two" front; 2 is assumed). Piston areas, the proportional control valve and the delay valve are not modelled. The 3-piston calipers front and rear are Honda data (specs JSON).
- **V4 engine voice** (`src/audio/v4-engine.ts`, `vehicle.audio.engine`). A procedural WebAudio voice: one `PeriodicWave` of exhaust pulses per 720° cycle at rpm/120 Hz, through a soft saturator and an rpm/load lowpass, plus band-passed intake noise under load. The firing angles come from the geometry (`vAngle`, `crankpin`): the vfr800's 90° V4 with a 180° crank fires at 0°, 90°, 270°, 540° (gaps 90-180-270-180), which gives the uneven V4 beat. Cars keep the shared engine note; no sample files; still no turbo and the soft gear click. Where a browser cannot build the voice the note plays instead.

**Top speed ~250 km/h (owner's bike, limiter).** Txema Vicente's own VFR800FI cuts out at about 250 km/h; this is the owner's report, not Honda data (`ownerReports` in the specs JSON). The preset declares `powertrain.speedLimiter: { kmh: 250 }`, a soft ignition-style cut: at 250 km/h the drive and engine load drop to zero until the speed is `hysteresisKmh` (default 2) lower, so the bike hovers at 248–250 km/h in sixth instead of hitting a wall. Gear ratios and final drive stay the Honda tooth counts; to make 250 reachable the two-wheeler drag placeholder `dragFactor` went from 0.3 to 0.18 (≈ CdA 0.29 m², TODO(unverified)). On the flat test scene the bike now does 0–100 km/h in ~3.8 s (unchanged), ~190 km/h after 10 s and reaches 245 km/h after ~21 s; without the limiter it would run on to ~262 km/h. Wheelies are unchanged (low speed).

## Phase 3: cockpit, automatic rider and Shift hooligan modes (implemented)

- **Automatic rider** (`autoRiderInput`, `twoWheeled.rider.auto`). With none of U / I / O / L pressed the rider hangs off into turns in proportion to lean and steering, moves forward under hard acceleration (keeps the front down), back under hard front braking (limits the stoppie) and sits centred when cruising, at the normal `rider.rate`. A key takes over within `takeover` (0.1 s); `releaseDelay` (1 s) after the keys are released the automatic rider blends back in over `blend`. Only key-driven sideways shifts add body steering. Every gain and delay is per preset; `auto.enabled: false` restores the phase-2 centred rider. Deliberate wheelies and stoppies still work: L + throttle (or the Shift modes) override it.
- **Full lean ("total estribo")** (`twoWheeled.pegLean`, timing in `twoWheeledDefaults.fullLean`). Normal riding keeps `maxLean` (0.7 rad, ~40°). Holding full steer once the turn is at that limit raises the lean limit over `seconds` (2 s, eased) to the peg lean of that side; letting the steer ease relaxes it back over `relaxSeconds` (1 s). On the way the turn may tighten by the hang-off angle so the bike itself (not just bike plus rider) reaches the peg, and the automatic rider hangs off further (`rider.auto.pegHangOff`, up to 35 % of `lateral` more). The bike never leans past the peg. At the peg it scrapes: the bullet-impact sparks stream from the touching point on the inside of the turn (about 30 bursts a second, trailing with the bike, more and faster with speed) and a light metallic grind plays (`MetalScrape`). The vfr800 values come from its GLB at static ride height (`node scripts/vfr800-lean-clearance.mjs`: roll about the wheel-plane contact line, as the game's chassis leans): left 0.9098 rad (52.1°), first touch the left footpeg rubber; right 0.9458 rad (54.2°), first touch the right passenger footrest (the right rider peg rubber at 56.8° and the silencer at 55.8° follow). With a round tyre crown (half the tyre width) the same parts touch at about 58°; the game uses the knife-edge values because its rigid chassis and ray-cast wheels lean about the contact line. At 100 km/h the lateral grip holds the full lean (about 1.4 g, a gameplay value).
- **Tuck behind the windscreen** (`twoWheeled.rider.tuck`). The automatic rider's head starts to go down at `kmh` (180 km/h) and reaches the full tuck at `fullKmh` (200 km/h) along an eased ramp, never a step. Slowing down it comes back up along the same ramp shifted `kmh − releaseKmh` (10 km/h) lower, so it is still fully tucked down to 190 and fully sat up at `releaseKmh` (170): hysteresis, no hunting at a steady speed. Hard braking (`brakeG`) sits the rider up at any speed. The forward key (I) keeps its normal range below 180 and reaches into the tuck along the same 180 → 200 ramp. At full tuck the cockpit eye is at `eye`; partial tucks blend towards it (−0.22 m down, 0.34 m forward on the vfr800; the same for the automatic tuck and a full manual tuck): behind the screen but high enough that the horizon clears the fairing, with the tachometer and cluster at the bottom of the view and the road through and above the screen (an earlier −0.32 / 0.44 eye was too deep to see the road ahead). `twoWheeledPose` adds `tuck`.
- **Shift hooligan modifier** (`twoWheeled.hooligan`). While Shift is held the wheelie and stoppie assists, the Dual CBS link and the automatic rider's fore-aft moves are off:
  - **Shift + W** from standstill or low speed (below `burnoutSpeed`, fading over `burnoutFade`): launch burnout. The rear spins up to `spinSpeed` above road speed (engine revs follow the wheel; tyre smoke and skid marks follow the slip), only `burnoutTraction` of the drive reaches the road and the tail wiggles a little (`slide`).
  - **Shift + L + W**: a much bigger wheelie with no assist. The rider holds the climb at about `wheelieRate` by feathering the throttle; it never stops climbing, so held too long it loops past `crashPitch` and the bike crashes (fallen until R).
  - **Shift + S + Space + W**: stationary burnout. Front locked, the bike held in place and upright, the rear spinning with heavy smoke and marks. With Shift held, W and S together give throttle plus lever instead of cancelling out (`PlayerInput.frontBrake`).
  - **Shift while braking with S**: stoppie. The rear lifts as the bike comes to a stop (`stoppieBrake`, rising at about `stoppieRate`); let go of Shift and the assist brings it down, hold it and the bike goes over the front and crashes.
  - Without Shift nothing changes. `twoWheeledPose` adds `rearWheelSpeed`, `roadSpeed`, `hooligan` and `crashed`. The rise rates are a gameplay stand-in for rider technique, not measured data.
- **Instrument cluster** (`src/render/vehicle-presentation/motorcycle-instruments.ts`, `vehicle.cluster`). Live canvas-texture gauges on GLB anchors: a small analogue speedometer on the left (0–280 km/h, TODO(unverified) scale), a larger white-faced tachometer in the centre (0–13,000 rpm, red zone from `powertrain.maxRpm`) and a portrait LCD on the right (clock in game time, gear, odometer and trip for the session). Both needles sweep during the start-up self-test. Green turn-signal tell-tales blink with Z / X through the light controller (the game has no hazard switch; the lamp logic accepts both sides). Four warning lamps along the bottom strip, left to right high beam (blue, K), oil pressure (red, only while cranking before the engine runs), neutral (green, N) and FI (amber, self-check during the start-up, then off). Only the "N" icon is legible in the GLB; the rest of the set is the most plausible one for this cluster and is configurable (`cluster.lamps`).
- **GLB cockpit pass** (`scripts/prepare-vfr800-cockpit.mjs`, reproducible). The mirrors were already modelled (housing, stalk, chrome glass) but merged into `Body` and rendered black; they are now `mirror_L` / `mirror_R` nodes under `Body` (fairing-mounted, they do not turn with the bars). The static needles, hubs and speedometer ticks are removed for the live gauges; anchors `gauge_speedo`, `gauge_tacho`, `gauge_lcd`, `lamp_signal_l|r`, `lamp_warning_1…4` are added (underscores: three.js strips dots from node names). The windscreen moves from transmission to alpha blending with a lighter smoked tint (opacity 0.26), so the cluster behind it stays visible. The exhaust material already was polished chrome (metallic 1, roughness 0.055, light base colour); it looked black because the scene has no environment map. `applyReflectionEnvironment` now gives two-wheelers' metallic and `reflective`-tagged materials a small procedural sky reflection (dimmed at night), so the exhaust, discs, mirror glass and screen read as chrome and glass. Cars are unchanged.
- **Rear-view mirrors.** The `mirror_L` / `mirror_R` glass (the `Reflector` mesh in each part) becomes a live mirror in the cockpit view, the same `CarMirrors` the cars use (`motorcycleMirrorLenses`). Vehículos › Espejos (the existing sliders) adjusts each glass live: _giro_ (yaw, + outward) ±15° and _inclinación_ (tilt, + up) ±10° in 0.5° steps, shown in degrees, with the values to bake. The default is 0° / 0° on both sides, the glass exactly as modelled (`mirrorTilt` defaults to 0 on two-wheelers). Bake chosen angles per preset in `vehicle.mirrorAim` (e.g. `"mirrorAim": {"left": {"yaw": 0, "tilt": 0}, "right": {"yaw": 0, "tilt": 0}}` in `vfr800.json`), or give a host default with `&mirrors={"vfr800":{…}}`.

### Preset values: sourced versus placeholder

Honda-sourced (see the specs JSON below): power 110 PS (81 kW) and 82 Nm peaks, the six gear ratios from tooth counts, overall final drive 64/33 × 43/17 ≈ 4.9055, wheelbase (1.44 m, also the GLB hub distance), overall size, tyre radii from nominal tyre sizes, the 234 kg US curb mass.

Placeholders, **not Honda data** (marked TODO(unverified) in `twoWheeledDefaults` in `src/config/simulation.ts` or listed here): total mass 310 kg (234 kg curb plus an assumed ~76 kg rider), the rider's seated centre of mass (`rider.seat`), colliders and the inertia they imply, `idleRpm` 1,200, `maxRpm` 11,500 (the end of the estimated torque table, not a rev limiter), `maxWheelForceN` 2,950, `grip`, suspension stiffness and damping, `maxLean` 0.7 rad, `fallLean` 1.15 rad, `balanceSpeed` 3 m/s, lean and assist response, steering rate, front/rear brake forces (72 / 9; the front capacity is above what the lever alone applies through CBS), every `pitchAssist`, `rider`, `clutchKick` and `cbs` value, tyre friction slip and the drag factor (0.18, tuned so the owner-reported 250 km/h limiter is reachable). The authored GLB steering axis gives a 24.4° rake (factory 25.5°); the controller uses the GLB axis so visuals and physics agree.

### Not implemented yet

A real clutch (the kick and the Shift modes are gameplay stand-ins), the CBS proportional control and delay valves, analogue throttle shaping for the keyboard, LODs, a visible rider body (only the head offset moves: the floating helmet avatar sits at the rider's head pose), rounded-tyre camber forces and countersteering dynamics.

## Technical reference

[Honda's 1999 ED brochure](https://www.vfritaliaclubforum.it/wp/files/tecnica/Storia%20VFR/VFR800FI-1998_1999.pdf) (PDF page 31) specifies the 781.7 cc, gear-driven DOHC V4: 81 kW at 10,500 rpm and 82 Nm at 8,500 rpm (DIN). The [1998–2001 Honda factory service manual](https://www.scribd.com/document/444334608/Honda-VFR800FI-Interceptor-98-01-Service-Manual-Www-manuale-reparatie-eu), General Information 1-4–1-6, supplies transmission tooth counts and steering geometry. These are scans of Honda publications, not specifications for the 2002-on VTEC model.

Machine-readable values live in [vfr800fi-1999.specs.json](../assets/library/motorcycles/vfr800fi-1999/vfr800fi-1999.specs.json). Ratios use tooth counts rather than corrupted OCR decimals: primary 64/33; gears 37/13, 33/16, 31/19, 28/21, 30/26, 29/28; final 43/17. There are six forward gears, neutral, no reverse, a hydraulic wet clutch and rear-wheel chain drive.

The JSON includes dry mass (208 kg), tank (21 L), dimensions, 1.440 m wheelbase, tyre/rim sizes, rake/trail, suspension and Dual CBS brakes. The US/Canada service-manual curb mass (234 kg) is a separate regional reference, not an ED-certified mass or a mass including the rider. Add rider, luggage and operating fluids explicitly; do not use dry mass as total simulated mass.

Factory 1999 ED front axle travel is 109 mm and rear travel 120 mm; the 1998 manual lists 120 mm front. The authored visual rig currently allows 100 mm compression. These values have different provenance and must be reconciled during integration. Nominal tyre radii are unloaded geometry, not measured loaded rolling radii.

## Power curve

The JSON and [CSV](../assets/library/motorcycles/vfr800fi-1999/vfr800fi-1999-power.csv) provide a provisional full-throttle crankshaft curve for simulation. **Intermediate points are authored estimates**, constrained to the published DIN peaks; they are not a measured dynamometer trace or a digitization of Honda's comparative graph. The alternative 1998 EC figures are recorded separately and are not mixed into this curve.

Interpolate torque linearly and calculate power as torque × rpm × 2π/60. The table covers 1,000–11,500 rpm; it does not specify idle, stall behavior, overrun or a rev limiter. Those unknowns, losses, engine inertia, tyre coefficients, suspension rates and aerodynamics are null rather than fabricated factory data. Rear wheel torque needs primary × selected gear × final reduction × measured/calibrated efficiency; the published curve is at the crankshaft. Road speed estimates also need loaded rolling radius and clutch/tyre slip.

## Remaining engine work (beyond phase 2)

Phases 1 and 2 cover the minimal parts of items 1, 2 (front/rear braking with a placeholder CBS split), 4 (rider-dependent centre of mass) and 5 (engine audio); everything beyond it below is still open.

1. Add a discriminated motorcycle definition and dedicated two-wheel controller using the existing physics world and fixed-step clock. Extend anchor extraction for wheel.front/rear, steering, fork and swingarm without weakening four-wheel validation.
2. Implement rounded-tyre contacts, normal loads and combined longitudinal/lateral grip, rear drive, clutch, sequential gears and engine braking. Model front/rear braking and the original Dual CBS coupling. The 1999 bike has no factory ABS; optional assistance must be explicit.
3. Simulate springs, damping and travel stops; front compression follows the inclined fork axis and rear motion follows the swingarm arc and shock motion ratio. Derive presentation from interpolated simulation snapshots.
4. Implement lean, countersteering, rider balance, low-speed assistance, falls, recovery and rider-dependent centre of mass/inertia. Balance assistance must be configurable and must not create fake wheel contacts.
5. Connect motorcycle inputs, start/stop, clutch/gears, rider cameras, engine audio and the shared AuthoredVehicleLights channels. Provide one lifecycle owner and release the cloned visual chain geometry on disposal.
6. Calibrate unknown mass distribution, suspension, tyre and powertrain parameters. Test straight-line stability, different-speed turns, braking, loss of grip, slopes, curbs, jumps, one-wheel contact, multiple bikes, resets and planetary gravity/rebase at a fixed step.

The GLB already separates steering, fork slider, front/rear wheels, swingarm, shock body/rod and chain. Steering is limited visually to ±35°. The visual helper does not generate forces or collision shapes. The planar discs and simplified drivetrain are visual choices, not physical geometry.
