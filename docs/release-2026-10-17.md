# Driving, startup and lighting update — 17 October 2026

This snapshot preserves the working Engine improvements developed on 16–17 October, with a companion Euskadi Online host PR. PC, VR and AR continue to share Engine; the new desktop HUD and credit choreography live in the host repository.

## Startup and first-use stalls

Preparation now awaits the actual terrain, textures, models, effects and shader work instead of silently proceeding after preload deadlines. Desktop startup prepares the visible area and drains building batches before reveal; XR retains its smaller nearby footprint. Camera warmup includes cockpit/first-person head turns and the available camera views. Audio graph preparation, texture uploads, mirrors, destruction, weapon effects and vehicle presentation are covered before gameplay.

The loading canvas exposes the current operation and frame-stall breakdown (CPU, render, physics, terrain and shader program changes), allowing remaining stalls to be investigated. There are no arbitrary credit waits. These changes address identified first-use work; they do not establish that all hardware-specific stalls are eliminated. Validate cold startup, head turns, acceleration, camera switching and collisions on the actual desktop GPU and Quest.

## Lighting and instruments

- Street lamps use a bounded nearest-light budget: 8 for the default/balanced setting, fewer below it and more above it. They follow dusk/dawn. Applying a new budget is explicit and warns about shader compilation.
- Street-lamp shadows are an opt-in development experiment. Sun shadows remain enabled above the minimum tier, with the effective resolution exposed in settings.
- The headlamp follows camera pitch and yaw, reuses a vehicle-light slot, and defaults to no shadows. Vehicles and pedestrians do not introduce an additional simultaneous headlamp.
- Cars have a subtle red rear road wash using a spare slot, without shadows. The latest tuning advances it 20 cm, widens the cone, preserves its 5 m reach and reduces intensity to 60% of the initial setting. It stays visible in cockpit mirrors.
- High beams keep a broad cone matching the configured dipped-beam width and aim horizontally. The right dipped beam is raised only 0.5 degrees relative to its authored aim; the left is unchanged. This supersedes the earlier request for a uniform downward adjustment.
- S3 instruments split unlit markings from luminous numbers/needles/display and clean the panel edge. Continuous S3 intake hiss is removed while the rest of the engine character remains.

The shared toolbar separates general and contextual vehicle controls, including signals, lights, horn and enter/exit. FOV defaults to zero offset and exposes degrees; manual resolution supports 25–500%. The host adds real-time/manual clock controls and date display.

## Follow-up: confirmed flashlight shader regression

The captured performance log showed 110 new gameplay shader programs, all with one spotlight shadow absent from the preload layout. The desktop settings menu restored the saved flashlight-shadow preference after `play()` completed. That late restore changed the lighting layout and caused first-use compilation across the main view and mirrors. Both desktop entry points now restore the preference before `play()` prepares those views; mounting the settings menu no longer changes the layout. Explicitly enabling shadows during play can still require shader compilation.

Detailed stall capture is now opt-in: append `perfLog=1` to the desktop URL before loading. This enables the last-100-record log, shader names/variants and render-pass attribution, the cell-install indicator and the host copy button. Normal startup does not record those logs or scan shader programs for attribution; FPS remain available. Render timings measure main-thread submission, not GPU execution time.

Vehicle preparation yields between model/material, texture, shader, scene and mirror phases, with progress updates rather than arbitrary timeouts. Individual driver calls can still block. The accompanying host removes the fade between loading-operation labels.

This follow-up also preserves the tested Z16 development refresh implementation and Home-key map inspector, plus recovery that rejects submerged roads and the catch floor. The refresh endpoint is opt-in; actual Atlas regeneration and seamless swapping still require integration acceptance. See the dedicated map and Z16 documentation.
